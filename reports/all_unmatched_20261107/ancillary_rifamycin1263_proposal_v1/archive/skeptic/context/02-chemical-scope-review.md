**Why these decisions are justified**

Polymyxin B is explicitly defined as a B1/B2 mixture by [NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/publications/dictionaries/cancer-drug/def/polymyxin-b). [ChEBI:8309](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:8309) denotes B1 and carries CAS `4135-11-9`. The local NCIT source assigns CAS `1404-26-8` to `NCIT:C61894`. This supports correcting the generic runtime route without changing MIM's current mixture identity.

The exact bare `rifamycin` tokens in the original `microbedecoder_database.zip/database.csv` occur in two metabolite-production records (BacDive 16969 and 13220) and one resistance record (BacDive 166282). None of those three fields specifies SV or a CAS. The extracted rows are saved in [rifamycin-source-occurrences.json](rifamycin-source-occurrences.json). NCIT's existing specific CAS and SV synonym therefore add specificity absent from the source. [ChEBI's SV entry](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:29673) confirms SV is a member of the rifamycin family. Family-level indexing must retain the unspecified source wording and must not infer resistance to, or production of, every family member.

[ChEBI:17712](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:17712) is explicitly a child of generic xanthine `CHEBI:15318` and carries CAS `69-89-6`. MIM's current record uses that CAS and chemical representation. Preserve this existing material evidence while allowing generic trait observations to retain generic scope. This is a canonical representation decision; matching a standard InChI does not independently establish a tautomer-pure reagent. Record the retained identifier difference as a reviewed context distinction in the promotion audit, with regressions for both lookup routes.

The [ATCC 416 medium sheet](https://www.atcc.org/-/media/product-assets/documents/microbial-media-formulations/4/1/6/atcc-medium-416.pdf?rev=b4280b8582cc4389a3bdf40c98a7fa03) lists Sorbitan Monooleate at 1 g/L without a CAS, stereochemical specification, or scratch-component supplier identifier. It also names premixed BD products, which does not by itself establish the scratch ingredient's identity. [JECFA](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/jecfa_additives/docs/Monograph1/Additive-432.pdf) defines the commercial sorbitan monooleate additive as a mixture, while [ChEBI:183688](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chebi/CHEBI:183688) depicts a specific molecule. The captured NCIT record supplies a molecular formula and molecular synonym but no definition establishing the mixture scope.

A further local-source conflict supports blocking automatic sorbitan equivalence: `kg-microbe/data/raw/foodon.owl`, at the imported `CHEBI:53426` class, includes the alias `sorbitan monooleate` and both Codex 433 and 494, although the class label is `polysorbate 80`. This is an upstream alias/additive-number conflation to review, not evidence that the two materials are identical. The inspected file includes ignored data. A local material record is a usable disposition while the exact identity is held for supplier or recipe-source clarification.
