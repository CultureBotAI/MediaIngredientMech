L71@P4: Overview
L72@P4: Limonene and its isomers were declared Priority Existing Chemicals for full
L73@P4: assessment on 1 August 2000 due to widespread use of the chemicals and their
L74@P4: reported effects in the liver and kidneys of animals after repeated exposure and
L75@P4: skin and eye irritation.
L76@P4: Greater than 100 tonnes of d-limonene is manufactured in Australia per year from
L77@P4: orange oils by extraction through distillation. d-Limonene, dipentene and products
L78@P4: containing limonene and its isomers (d-, l-, dl-) are also imported into Australia at
L79@P4: about 1600 tonnes a year. The concentration of limonene in products varies
L80@P4: widely, from very low to 90%.
L81@P4: d-Limonene and dipentene are mainly used in the formulation of a range of
L82@P4: fragrance or flavour blends that are used in the further formulation of end products
L83@P4: for industry and consumer use and as food additives. They are also used in the
L84@P4: formulation of a large range of industrial and consumer products, mainly cleaning
L85@P4: and degreasing products, as replacements for chlorinated solvents. Some pure dlimonene and dipentene are also used directly in cleaning and degreasing,
L86@P4: removing wax, microbiological and gemstone testing and as a general reagent in
L87@P4: laboratories.
L88@P4: Limonene products are either used directly or further diluted or mixed with other
L89@P4: components by industrial end users. Industrial products containing limonene are
L90@P4: mainly hand cleaners, industrial cleaning/degreasing products and removers and
L91@P4: strippers. The final concentration of limonene in industrial products varies widely
L92@P4: and ranges from < 1% to 95%. The major use of limonene in consumer products is
L93@P4: as flavouring and/or fragrance agents in food, pharmaceuticals and household and
L94@P4: cosmetic products. Limonene is also present in essential oils that are used widely
L95@P4: in Australia. The concentration of limonene in the final consumer products also
L96@P4: varies largely and is predominantly low  1%, but can be as high as 70%.
L97@P4: Occupational exposure may occur during the use, transportation and disposal of
L98@P4: limonene or limonene products. A high potential for worker exposure during use
L99@P4: of limonene or limonene products has been identified due to widespread end uses,
L100@P4: modes of applications and lack of control measures at some worksites. Workers
L101@P4: are likely to be exposed by skin and eye contact during manual operations and
L102@P4: cleaning of equipment, repacking, formulation and end use. There is also a
L103@P4: potential for inhalation exposure, especially during use of limonene or limonene
L104@P4: products in confined spaces and in places with limited ventilation, heated blending
L105@P4: processes, high speed mechanical stirring, worksites with an open mixing process
L106@P4: and no exhaust ventilation, and certain modes of applications such as spraying.
L107@P4: Deliberate skin contact occurs during use of hand cleaners containing limonene.
L108@P4: Limonene is a flammable liquid and explosive vapour/air mixtures may be formed
L109@P4: at temperatures above 48C. Its packing group (III) indicates that flammability risk
L110@P4: is in the lower range. Rags or other combustible material that have been dipped or
L111@P4: soaked in limonene may spontaneously combust. Distillation to dryness may also
L112@P4-5: lead to concentration of peroxides and the risk of explosion. Limonene vii
L113@P5: d-Limonene is readily absorbed by inhalation and ingestion. Dermal absorption is
L114@P5: reported to be lower than by the inhalation route. d-Limonene is rapidly distributed
L115@P5: to different tissues in the body, readily metabolised and eliminated primarily
L116@P5: through the urine.
L117@P5: Limonene exhibits low acute toxicity by all three routes in animals. Limonene is a
L118@P5: skin irritant in both experimental animals and humans. Limited data are available
L119@P5: on the potential to cause eye and respiratory irritation. Autoxidized products of dlimonene have the potential to be skin sensitisers. Limited data are available in
L120@P5: humans on the potential to cause respiratory sensitisation.
L121@P5: Autoxidation of limonene occurs readily in the presence of light and air forming a
L122@P5: variety of oxygenated monocyclic terpenes. Risk of skin sensitisation is high in
L123@P5: situations where contact with oxidation products of limonene occurs.
L124@P5: Renal tumours induced by limonene in male rats are thought to be sex and species
L125@P5: specific and are not considered relevant to humans. Repeated exposure affects the
L126@P5: amount and activity of liver enzymes, liver weight, blood cholesterol levels and
L127@P5: bile flow in animals. Increase in liver weight is considered a physiological
L128@P5: adaptation as no toxic effects on the liver have been reported. From the data
L129@P5: available, it is not possible to identify a NOAEL for these effects. Limonene is
L130@P5: neither genotoxic nor teratogenic nor toxic to the reproductive system.
L131@P5: Occupational risk assessment identified a number of areas of concern. Risk of
L132@P5: skin, eye and respiratory irritation exists when working in a confined space during
L133@P5: cleaning of a storage tank for repacking. These risks may also occur during end
L134@P5: use of pure limonene and/or products containing high concentrations of limonene,
L135@P5: especially when used in confined spaces and in places with limited ventilation as
L136@P5: well as by certain application modes such as spraying or where personal protective
L137@P5: equipment (PPE) is not used.
L138@P5: The risk of health effects following repeated exposure is likely to be high during
L139@P5: end use of limonene in confined spaces and in places with limited ventilation.
L140@P5: Significant environmental releases are expected to the aquatic compartment as a
L141@P5: result of the use pattern in cleaners. However, as a result of dilution, degradation
L142@P5: and evaporation these releases are not expected to result in adverse effects on
L143@P5: aquatic organisms. No studies were identified on chronic effects, and therefore
L144@P5: risks associated with chronic exposures of aquatic organisms to limonene in
L145@P5: "polluted" waters cannot be determined.
L146@P5: Due to the high volatility of limonene the atmosphere is expected to be the major
L147@P5: environmental sink for this chemical, where it is expected to rapidly undergo gasphase reactions with photochemically produced hydroxyl radicals, ozone, and
L148@P5: nitrate radicals. The oxidation of limonene may contribute to aerosol and
L149@P5: photochemical smog formation. Ozonolysis of limonene may also lead to the
L150@P5: formation of hydrogen peroxide and organic peroxides, which have various toxic
L151@P5: effects on plant cells and may be part of the damage to forests observed in the last
L152@P5: decades. However, limonene has not been identified as an air toxic in Australia
L153@P5: and is not on the list of substances reported to the National Pollutant Inventory.
L154@P5: Potential hazards relevant to public health are skin irritancy and sensitisation from
L155@P5: use of consumer products, varying with the concentration of limonene in the
L156@P5-6: product and, for sensitisation, with its oxidation status.Priority Existing Chemical viii
L157@P6: In Australia transport and occupational use of limonene is controlled through a
L158@P6: number of national standards and codes, corresponding State and Territory
L159@P6: legislation and workplace controls. Limonene is not listed in the Standard for the
L160@P6: Uniform Scheduling of Drugs and Poisons. It has not been identified as an air
L161@P6: toxic in Australia and is not on the list of substances reported to the National
L162@P6: Pollutant Inventory.
L163@P6: The major recommendations of the report focus on the autoxidation potential of
L164@P6: limonene. As well as the current classification as a skin irritant, it is recommended
L165@P6: that an additional risk phrase on skin sensitisation (R43 – May cause sensitisation
L166@P6: by skin contact) be taken up in the National Occupational Health and Safety
L167@P6: Commission (NOHSC) List of Designated Hazardous Substances. A range of
L168@P6: measures is recommended to minimise autoxidation of limonene throughout its life
L169@P6: cycle. Workplaces are asked to consider the hazards of spontaneous combustion,
L170@P6: and of peroxides in distillation. Addition of oxidised limonene to the list of
L171@P6: substances used in allergy testing is recommended.
L172@P6: As commercial limonene / dipentene is supplied under several Chemical Abstracts
L173@P6: Service (CAS) numbers, suppliers are asked to ensure that hazard classification of
L174@P6: these materials reflects the limonene content. Classification for aspiration hazard is
L175@P6: also required under the NOHSC Approved Criteria for Classifying Hazardous
L176@P6: Substances where the material meets viscosity and surface tension criteria. Other
L177@P6: recommendations cover workplace controls, hazard communication and
L178@P6-7: consideration of minor changes in transport coding.Limonene ix
